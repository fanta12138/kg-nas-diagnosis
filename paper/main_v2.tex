%%==================================%% Abstract
\abstract{Two decisions set the accuracy of an anomaly detector: which network to
deploy, and how the per-pixel map becomes the accept/reject score. The second is
made silently --- an unweighted mean over all pixels --- and it is the one that
matters. Changing only that reduction, on trained models, raises mean AUROC from
82.80\% to 90.21\% on MVTec-AD and from 82.35\% to 90.57\% on VisA, and lifts a
below-chance category from 42.22\% to 84.01\%, with no retraining or labels.
It follows a dilution law: the mean scales with defect area, top-$k$ with the tail
floor, so it holds only while the defect covers under a tenth of the image and
fades where the default saturates. The architecture axis contributes 0.54\% of the
variance in accuracy against 95.32\% for the category, two thirds of the 42-cell
search space cannot reach the deployed model, and twelve searches all returned a
collapse-prone backbone. The benchmark cannot adjudicate either: against a
0.5-point margin, the standard comparison resolves 2.98. We report a pipeline at
82.47\% where published methods reach 99\%, and show where the missing points are,
and are not.}

\keywords{Industrial visual inspection, Unsupervised anomaly detection, Neural
architecture search, Knowledge distillation, Score fusion, Proxy reliability}
