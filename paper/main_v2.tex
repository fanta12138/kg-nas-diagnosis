A Diagnostic Study of Neural Architecture Search and Score Fusion in

%%==================================%% Abstract
\abstract{Unsupervised anomaly detection models normality so that inspection can
run on conforming parts alone. Two design decisions determine whether such a
system works: the network architecture, and the reduction of per-pixel
discrepancies to the image-level score that accepts or rejects a part. The
literature invests in the first; the second is rarely documented. We measure both,
and first what the benchmark can measure at all. On MVTec-AD the contested margin
between successive state-of-the-art generations is $0.5$ points, while the
standard single-category comparison resolves $2.98$ --- larger than the entire
spread of the architectures it ranks. Two thirds of the search space is
deployment-inert: the searched feature dimension never reaches the deployed
detector, and the distillation signal that ranks candidates separates the twelve
well-behaved backbones by only $5.7\%$ of its median, the same order as its own
noise, while returning exactly zero for two of them --- which the minimising rule
then prefers. Training 210 detectors, every backbone on every category under one
protocol, attributes $0.54\%$ of the variance in accuracy to the architecture and
$95.32\%$ to the category, and the backbone ordering does not transfer between
categories. Twelve independent searches consequently all returned a
collapse-prone backbone, performing worse than uniform random selection. The
leverage lies instead in the reduction step: replacing the unweighted mean over
the discrepancy map with top-$k$ pooling raises mean AUROC from $82.36\%$ to
$90.28\%$ across three seeds without retraining, and the policy can be chosen
without any anomaly label. The pipeline reaches $82.47\%$ against published values
of $95.6$--$99.6\%$; we report that gap rather than obscure it.}

\keywords{Industrial visual inspection, Unsupervised anomaly detection, Neural
architecture search, Knowledge distillation, Score fusion, Proxy reliability}
